Software updates

    Restart reminders

    Restarting clears spyware and other bad code that hides only in the phone’s memory, and installs waiting updates. Security agencies advise restarting regularly. Apps aren’t allowed to restart a phone by themselves, so BlackSwan reminds you at 5:00 am and 7:00 pm every day.

    How to restart
    • Most Android phones: press and hold the power button (or power + volume up on newer Samsung and Pixel phones), then tap Restart.
    • iPhone: press and hold the side button and either volume button, slide to power off, wait 30 seconds, then hold the side button until the Apple logo appears.

    Protection against spyware and snooping

    Zero-click attacks (like the LANDFALL spyware sent as pictures over WhatsApp), “man-in-the-middle” snooping on your internet, and fake mobile towers that grab your phone’s identity (IMSI and IMEI). BlackSwan checks what it can; tick off the rest as you do them.

      Processors and firmware

      The application processor runs Android and your apps. The baseband (modem) processor runs the mobile radio with its own firmware, which apps can’t inspect directly; BlackSwan shows its firmware version so you can check it’s up to date.

      Apps with powerful access

      Apps that can read your screen, your notifications or texts, control the phone, or that weren’t installed from an app store. Most are fine; remove any you don’t recognise.

      • Run a check to see results.

      Phone service codes

      Codes you type into the Phone dialler to verify your phone and check if your calls are being diverted. Tap a code to copy it, then paste it into the keypad. These are for Android / Samsung; on an iPhone only *#06# works.

      • IMEI & serial. Check it at samsung.com/support/imei — it must match your model. Also use it to report a lost or stolen phone.
      • Firmware (AP / CP / CSC). Confirms a genuine build and your region. A fake phone shows gibberish or nothing.
      • Hardware test menu. A real Samsung opens a full screen / sensor test; a counterfeit usually can’t. Just exit when done.
      • Call-forwarding check. Shows if your calls, texts or data are being diverted (a SIM-swap trick). It does not detect spyware.
      • Unreachable forwarding. The number used when your phone is off — should be your voicemail, not a strange number.
      • Cancel all forwarding. Clears any diversion you find. Safe for normal use.

      Never dial a code you don’t recognise.

      Some codes wipe the phone instantly with no warning (for example *2767*3855# or *#*#7780#*#*). Never enter a code someone messages you or a website tells you to, and never tap a tel: link from a stranger.

      No dial code can detect modern spyware — anyone claiming a “secret code” shows if you’re hacked is wrong. These codes verify your phone; the checks above protect it.

            Why

              Intrusion monitor

              Learns what is normal for you — your towers, Wi-Fi access points, routers and DNS — and warns when something changes the way fake towers, evil-twin hotspots and man-in-the-middle attacks do. Run it now and then, especially in hotels, airports and cafés.

                Mobile network

                Checks which network your phone is using. 2G is old and its encryption is weak; fake mobile towers (“IMSI catchers”) often force phones down to 2G.

                Computer on the same network? Check for ARP spoofing

                On a laptop or desktop, open a terminal, run arp -a and paste the result. One device claiming several addresses — especially your router’s — means someone is in the middle.

                  Certificate pinning

                  On a network you trust, copy a site’s SHA-256 certificate fingerprint (padlock → certificate) and pin it. On public Wi-Fi, paste it again: a different certificate means stop and check. Sites renew certificates every few months, so a mismatch is a warning, not proof.

                    ARTEX Shield

                    ARTEX is an AI attack framework: agents scan, guess passwords, hunt leaked secrets on GitHub and break in within minutes. It and its offshoots (such as AntiFox) have been used against banks, governments and software firms around the world since August 2026. BlackSwan looks for its traces in your logs: its user agents and known servers, its TLS fingerprint, its “burst, then think” request rhythm, password spraying and stolen-app-secret sign-ins.

                      Leaked secrets check

                      ARTEX’s fastest way in is a password, token or API key left in a public repository. Paste a file, config or commit diff to check it. Matches are masked on screen. Any secret that was ever public must be replaced (rotated), even if the commit was deleted. To scan whole repositories including their history, use the desktop agent: python3 blackswan_agent.py --secrets PATH

                        Block list

                        1. Keep any testing console off the internet: bind port 8787 to localhost and reach it through a VPN or SSH tunnel.
                        2. Turn on secret scanning and push protection for your repositories, use short-lived tokens, and never share credentials between test and live systems.
                        3. Rate-limit sign-in, password-reset and API endpoints; ARTEX campaigns ran thousands of guesses a minute.
                        4. Run the desktop agent with --artex on your network to find exposed ARTEX consoles and booby-trapped copies.
